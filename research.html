Working Papers

  1. Hidden Liquidity — Bond ETFs versus Stock ETFs
    View draft

    We examine the trading dynamics in exchange-traded funds (ETFs) with regard to hidden trading activity. Specifically, we compare the level of hidden liquidity in equity ETFs and bond ETFs. We find hidden volume in bond ETFs to be significantly higher than in equity ETFs (41% versus 28%). Using high frequency data, we find that trading costs (quoted, effective, and realized spread and price impact) and pinging activity are lower for bond ETFs than equity ETFs, yet hidden liquidity is higher in bond ETFs. Results are robust to out-of-sample tests and are not driven by a specific exchange or specific types of bond ETFs.

  2. Corporate Bond Trading around Unscheduled Corporate Events
    View draft

    This paper examines how corporate bonds trade as a response to corporate events that usually come as a surprise to the market. Such unscheduled corporate events include CEO turnovers, dividend changes, mergers and acquisitions (M&As), stock repurchases, seasoned equity offerings (SEOs), spin-offs, stock splits, or ticker symbol changes. Although some events experienced an increase in trading activity, some events did not drastically change the trading activity of their firms' bonds. Overall, bond traders react to dividend change announcements, M&A announcements, repurchase announcements, SEO announcements, and spin-off events, but not to CEO turnover events, stock splits, and ticker changes.

  3. Convertible Bond Trading
    Sole-authored · View draft

    I investigate how convertible bonds trade when the option to convert the convertible bond is in-the-money. Empirical results show trading activity in convertible bonds increases when the option is in-the-money. I examine whether in-the-money convertible bonds lead to a spillover effect on a firm's other bonds and find an increase in trading activity in those other bonds. I explore the equity market's reactions to when convertible bonds are convertible and find increases in trading activity and hidden liquidity.
